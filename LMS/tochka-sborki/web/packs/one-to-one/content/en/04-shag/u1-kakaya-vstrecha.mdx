<Phase type="concept">

## The “one to a few” step

The next step of the ladder is two to seven people. Attention here is almost personal: everyone is seen and heard. This is the nearest step, not a leap to a group of fifteen.

## Three forms

**A small group of your clients.** A few people with a similar request at one meeting run by your method. It fits if many steps got “yes” in module 02.

**A couple or a family.** Two or three close people in one session. It fits if your clients' requests are often about a relationship with someone else.

**Apprenticeship.** A small cycle for those who want to learn the method themselves. On the ladder this format stands on the “few” step: a cycle, in person. It fits if clients ask “how do you do it”.

## What research says

A 2016 meta-analysis of 67 psychotherapy studies compared group and individual formats. With the same protocol, the result was the same.

In coaching, the studies are small, and the data disagree. In a 2025 study with 79 physicians, six online meetings in a small group reduced burnout, and the difference from one to one was not significant. In a 2016 study with students, individual coaching did more for goals, and 39 percent left the group against 15.

There is no systematic review of group coaching. For couples and apprenticeship our review found no sources — there is only a worked example.

**In psychotherapy a group is no worse in result. In coaching there is little data, and a group is not for everyone.** The choice stays with you and the client.

## A worked example

In module 02, all six of the leader's steps got “yes” or “with a change”. Two clients asked about a group. She chooses a small group of her clients.
