<Phase type="activation">

Picture your favourite café. You have your own table in the corner. One day there is a notice on the door: “Shared tables on Thursdays”.

**What will you feel when you read it?** Think about what would make you stay a regular.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the café door. You will stay if the corner table waits for you on other days. And Thursday is an invitation, not a replacement.

**A client hears news of a group in one of two ways: “I am being handed over” or “I am being offered more”.**

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## What the client loses and what they gain

**They lose** all the attention on them alone. This is the strength of the “one to one” step, and there is no need to play it down.

**They gain** other people with the same thing. Reviews of group coaching put it this way: a person feels less alone, and the group keeps them in rhythm.

## Who stays one to one

Those who need privacy, depth and their own pace stay. Those whose work lies in the “no” steps from module 02 stay, such as hands-on work. Clients from the “what is outside my competence” column stay: they need a specialist, not a group.

**A higher step is not better than a lower one.** Part of the practice stays one to one, and that is fine.

## Independence is a goal, not a threat

The Global Code of Ethics asks you to support the client's independence. If a client moves into a group and copes there, the practice has done its job.

## Each their own conversation

A mailing saying “we now have a group” sounds like “I am being handed over”. Each client needs their own conversation: what changes for them and what stays as it was.

## A worked example

The leader talks to three clients in different ways.

To a long-standing client she offers the group as an addition: the one-to-one sessions stay. To a new one, as an invitation to start with the group. To the third she says their hands-on work stays one to one only.

<SelfCheck id="c3"/>

</Phase>

<Phase type="practice">

Go back to “A map of your practice” and to “The plan of the first step”.

1. Mark in the map which clients you will invite, to whom you will offer the group as an addition, and who will stay one to one.
2. For three different clients, think over the first sentence of the conversation.
3. Write into the plan who stays one to one and why.
4. Decide when you will tell them: before the group is recruited or after.

Usually before is better: the client hears the news from you, not from others.

</Phase>
