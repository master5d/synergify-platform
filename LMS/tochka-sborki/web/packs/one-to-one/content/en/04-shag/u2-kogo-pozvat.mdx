<Phase type="activation">

Picture a dinner host seating her guests. She has twenty friends, and the table has six places.

**What happens if she invites all twenty?** Think about what decides whether the evening works.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the table. An evening works not when the table is full, but when the guests suit each other.

**The guest list is already half the dinner.** It is the same with a first meeting for 2–7 people.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## Where participants come from

A practitioner usually has three sources:
- current clients with a similar request;
- their friends who have asked about you;
- the waiting list.

## Selection

Reviews of group coaching say it plainly: the group format is not for everyone, and participants are selected in advance. Rules and confidentiality are agreed at the first meeting.

One review advises recruiting participants from different teams, so that people feel freer to talk. For a practitioner this means: think about who knows whom and what they will want to say in front of each other.

## Three criteria

Three criteria are enough for a first meeting.

1. **A similar request.** People have something to share.
2. **Readiness to hear others.** Not everyone wants to share time.
3. **No signs from the lesson on the edge of competence.** Such clients need a specialist, not a group.

## Open or closed

In an open group, newcomers end up on an unequal footing. A closed group makes people wait until everyone is recruited. For a first meeting a closed group is simpler: the same people from start to finish.

## A worked example

The practitioner has five people on her waiting list and two clients who asked about a group. She invites four: two clients with a similar request — back tension against a background of stress, one person from the list, and a client's friend.

She does not invite one more client: that client shows signs from the lesson on the edge.

## Not everyone at once

A first meeting is not a mailing saying “everyone come”. **The more precise the list, the better the chance that the meeting becomes a beginning, not a one-off experience.**

<Phase type="practice">

Go back to the “The plan of the first step” template.

1. Write down everyone you could invite, from the three sources.
2. Put your three selection criteria into words.
3. Keep two to seven people on the list and write for each why exactly them.
4. Check the list against the “what is outside my competence” column in “A map of your practice”.

If fewer than two fit, that is an answer too: it is too early for the step.

</Phase>
