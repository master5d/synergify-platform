<Phase type="activation">

Picture a dinner at a counter where the chef cooks for one guest. He watches the guest eat and changes the next dish on the fly. Now six people sit down at the same counter.

**The chef is the same. What has changed in his work?** Think not about the food but about his attention, the price of the dinner and his voice.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the counter. With one guest, the chef adjusts to every bite. With six, he sets a shared rhythm, and the price of the dinner per person falls.

**The chef has not become worse or better. The format has changed, and a format has its own laws.** Your work with a client is a format too, not just “I work”.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## Three axes of the ladder

The academy's format ladder describes any leader's work by three axes.

**Step** — how many people are in front of you: from one to hundreds.
**Channel** — in person, online or hybrid.
**Event class** — one-off, regular or a cycle.

One-to-one sessions stand on the bottom step: one to one, in person and online, regular.

## The laws of the “one to one” step

Every step has its own laws. This one has five.

- **Attention.** All the attention goes to one person.
- **Price per person.** The highest, but there are few people.
- **Team.** Not needed.
- **The leader's voice.** It sounds in every sentence, and the person hears it just for themselves.
- **What the step gives.** Here you test your approach on a live person and learn to listen.

## A step, not a waiting room

People often think one to one is temporary, until you grow into a group. The ladder says otherwise: **a higher step is not better than a lower one; you can work on any of them for years.**

The course asks a different question. Is your one-to-one practice a step you can move up from? Or a ceiling you are pressing against? To answer, you first need to see it as a map.
