<Phase type="activation">

Picture a mountain guide. They lead a group along a familiar route. On the climb, one person starts to feel pain in the chest.

**When does a good guide stop leading the group on?** Think about what their craft is at that very minute.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the slope. The guide turns the person back down and calls the rescuers. They do not try to treat anyone.

**A guide who knows where the route ends has not failed at the job. That is the job.** It is the same with a client: referring in time is part of the practice.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

This is not medical or legal advice. The sources below are as of 2026-10. Check the rules of your profession for your country.

## What the codes require

The codes of professional associations say the same thing in different words.

- **Global Code of Ethics:** work within the limit of your competence and refer the client to a more suitable specialist where appropriate.
- **NCBTMB (massage and bodywork):** acknowledge the limitations of your work and refer clients to appropriate health professionals.
- **Yoga Alliance:** do not give advice or services you are not licensed for. You may give lists of local specialists and help services.

## Signs to stop

The most detailed signs come from the ICF guide on referring a client to therapy (2018).

- For weeks the problem has been getting in the way of daily life: sleep, work, home, relationships.
- The work is not moving: the client comes back to the same thing and only wants to talk it out.
- You see several signs of distress at once, not one.
- Signals in you: “this is more than I can handle”, anxiety before a session, the client depends on you too much.

The guide itself warns: these descriptions are not for diagnosis. **Your job is to notice and refer, not to diagnose.**

## How to say it

In private and without hurry. Start from your care for the client. Name what you see, concretely. Ask how they feel about the idea of turning to a specialist. The decision stays theirs, and the door to the conversation stays open.

If the work does not move without a specialist's help, you can honestly suggest a pause until therapy begins.

If there is danger right now, call the local emergency service and stay with the person until help arrives.

## Through the client's eyes

To a client, a referral may sound like “I am being turned away”. That is why care and an open door matter.

**Referring a client is not admitting weakness.** It is the same care as a session.

Open the “The edge of your competence” template.

1. Find the emergency number of your country and fill it in.
2. Fill in the referral list: at least three profiles — psychotherapist, doctor, crisis line — with contacts.
3. Go back to “A map of your practice”. In the “what is outside my competence” column, mark for each client what you would refer them on with.
