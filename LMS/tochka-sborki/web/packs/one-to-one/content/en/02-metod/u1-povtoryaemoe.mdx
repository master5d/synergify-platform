<Phase type="activation">

Picture your grandmother's pie recipe on a card. “Flour — as much as the dough takes. Salt by eye. Bake until it is right.”

**What can a grandchild repeat from this card, and what not?** Think about where the recipe is on it, and where the grandmother's hand is.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the card. The grandchild will repeat the order of steps and the ingredients. “By eye” and “until it is right” stayed in the grandmother's hand.

**Both parts make the pie. But only one moves to another kitchen.** A session is the same: it has a recipe, and it has your hand.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## Two parts of a session

**The method** — what repeats in the same form even without you. The order of steps, exercises, words, questions.

**The personal part** — what rests on you. Tone, intuition, the history with a particular client, adjusting with your hands.

Both parts are needed. The personal part is not a flaw of the method but your share in it. Yet only the method carries over to two people, a group or an apprentice.

## The apprentice test

A simple question helps to separate the parts. If an apprentice replaced you tomorrow, what could they repeat?

What they could is the method. What “you have to feel” is the personal part.

## A worked example

A practitioner laid out her last three sessions. In all three, these repeated:
- starting with the breath, five minutes;
- the question “what have you come with today”;
- a body scan;
- one exercise for the shoulders and neck;
- the question “what did you notice”;
- a short exercise to take home.

The personal part was something else: which exercise to choose today, hands-on work, a joke about Mondays.

## The living part and the skeleton

People often say: “My method cannot be described — it is alive.” **The living part is exactly the personal one. The method is the skeleton the living part rests on.** You can describe the skeleton without killing what is alive.

<Phase type="practice">

Recall your last three sessions with different clients.

1. Write down what you did in each, in order.
2. Underline what repeated in all three.
3. Split everything into two columns: the method and the personal part.
4. Check against “A map of your practice”: does the “method” column match what clients get from the method?

The “method” column becomes the basis of the next lesson.

</Phase>
