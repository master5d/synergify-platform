<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the sheet of music. The notes and the rests are written down. The touch on the keys and the musician's breath are not.

**The sheet lets someone else play the piece and does not take the author's playing away.** A method on one page does the same for a session.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## A session step

One step is three things: **an action, why it is there, and how many minutes.** Without the “why”, a step turns into a ritual. Without minutes, the session sprawls.

A session usually fits into four to seven steps. Fewer means something is missing. More means the steps are mixed with the personal part.

## One page is the limit

Module 01 had a sign of “too early”: you cannot explain your approach in five minutes. One page is exactly those five minutes.

If it did not fit, the method is not yet separated from you. Go back to the “personal” column and move the extra there.

## A worked example

The leader described her 50-minute session in six steps.

| # | Action | Why | Minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Starting with the breath | To slow down and come into the body | 5 |
| 2 | The question “what have you come with” | To find the request of the day | 10 |
| 3 | A body scan | To see where the tension is | 10 |
| 4 | An exercise for the request | To shift the state | 15 |
| 5 | The question “what did you notice” | To fix it in her own words | 5 |
| 6 | An exercise to take home | To carry the step away | 5 |

Everything that did not get into the table is her personal part: choosing the exercise, hands-on work, tone.

## More detail is not better

Three pages with notes for each client are no longer a method but an instruction for one person. **A method is shorter than the practice, and that is its strength.**
