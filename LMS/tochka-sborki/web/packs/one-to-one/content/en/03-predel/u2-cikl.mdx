Go back to the pool. A membership can last for years, and no one will ask what you have learned. The course ends, and at the end you can see: do you swim or not.

**A cycle has an end, and the end comes with the question “did it work”.** Endless meetings put that question off.

<Phase type="concept">

## Regular and a cycle

On the ladder, the event class has three values: one-off, regular, cycle. One-to-one sessions are usually regular: every week, with no end date.

A cycle is a number of meetings, a frequency and a goal named in advance. In coaching research, cycles have an end. For example, six one-hour sessions over four months, or eight weekly meetings over three months.

## Length and the bond

The Global Code of Ethics for coaches and mentors asks that the length of the work match its goals. **Endless meetings with no goal feed the bond from module 01.**

A cycle does not cancel regularity. After a cycle ends, you can start the next one — but with a new question.

## Rescheduling and cancellations

There is no open data on the share of cancellations among coaches and bodywork practitioners. Count from your own booking log.

A cancellation rule is named in advance, in words, before the first session. For example: a cancellation less than a day ahead is paid for. Which rule to choose is up to you. What matters is that it is named.

## A worked example

On average, the practitioner has one unpaid cancellation a week. The revenue falls from 1200 to 1140 — minus 5 percent.

The price of a working hour: 1140 divided by 28, about 41. There are still 28 hours: the time for the cancelled session was taken.

## “My clients don't cancel”

Until a rule is named, cancellations are invisible: they are “simply moved”, and nobody fills the freed hour. **An invisible cancellation is a lost hour too.**

<Phase type="practice">

Go back to the “The limit, worked out” template and to your booking log.

1. Count the unpaid cancellations over the last two months and divide by the number of weeks.
2. Put this number in the cancellations line and recalculate the revenue of the week and the price of a working hour.
3. Describe your cycle: how many meetings, how often, with what goal. If there is no cycle, write that down.
4. Put the rule for rescheduling and cancelling into one sentence.

Think about how you would tell this rule to a client who has come to you for a year.
