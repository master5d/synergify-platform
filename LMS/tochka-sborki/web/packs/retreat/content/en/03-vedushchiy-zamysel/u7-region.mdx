<Phase type="concept">

## The world is wider than the usual spots

A leader often picks a place by habit: where they have been, or where everyone goes. The retreat map of the world is wider. It has places of different character, and each draws its own people.

## Five kinds of places

- **Mountains with a sacred reputation** — Mount Shasta, Altai, the Sacred Valley of the Incas. Height, space, silence, the fame of a "place of power". They fit silence, meditation, personal change. They clash with loud formats and with people who need city comfort.
- **Deserts and red rocks** — Sedona, Baja California. Bright light, an open horizon, strong views. They fit body practices, creativity, nature. They clash with people who dislike heat and tourist crowds.
- **Tropical islands and coasts** — Bali, Koh Phangan, Sri Lanka, Tulum. Warmth, the sea, a yoga culture, many retreats nearby. They fit yoga, recovery from burnout, newcomers. They clash with silence: there is often a party around.
- **Old mountain and pilgrimage lands** — the Carpathians, Dagestan, Crimea. Ancient paths, local traditions, food, a long history. They fit nature, creativity, teams. They clash with people looking for the "exotic".
- **The edge of the world** — Antarctica. Extreme remoteness and the scale of nature. It fits personal change and leadership. It clashes with almost everyone who needs predictability.

This is not a ranking. The same place fits one theme and clashes with another.

## Narrative and fact

Many places come with stories: "energy", "vortexes", "ancient cities under the mountain". That is narrative — what visitors believe, and part of why they come. You can respect narrative and take it into account when you choose your audience, but do not present it as fact in a booklet or on a sign-up page.

A sales text about a place almost always talks about energy and stays silent on the simple things: who lives here and how a guest gets there. Read such texts separating what can be checked from what is told.

## Whose land it is

Many retreat places have Indigenous peoples and local residents for whom the place is sacred or simply home. Find out whose land it is and what they ask of visitors from their own sources. Do not retell other people's sacred stories in your programme as "the legend of the place".

## The technical side is the next step

Permits, season, roads, the venue and prices are retreat preparation, not the choice of a region. They are covered in "Logistics" (04/u4) and module 05 "Venue: the package, the occupancy, the payback". First decide whether the place fits the idea, then check whether you can run it there.

<Phase type="practice">

1. Write down the theme of your retreat and your audience — one line each.
2. Name three different candidate regions: from different kinds of places.
3. For each, write one sentence "why it fits" and one "where it will clash".
4. Open [retreat regions](https://academy.synergify.com/en/instrumenty/regiony/) and check at least one candidate with a guide: choose your theme and audience and look at the result.
5. Keep one region and write down which technical question to check first.

</Phase>
