<Phase type="activation">

Picture two rulers laid side by side. Each is marked in days before arrival. The venue drew one, and you drew the other, for your guests.

**Slide them with your eyes until the zeros line up.** Look at where one ruler is already in the red zone while the other is still green.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the two rulers. The venue's red zone is the days when you already pay for a cancellation. The guest's green zone is the days when a guest can still take their money back.

**If your green zone reaches into the venue's red zone, you pay the difference.** Every day of that overlap is your personal risk.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## How venues charge for cancellation

As of 2026-10, the typical picture in group contracts is this. While arrival is more than 90–120 days away, cancelling costs the first payment or nothing at all. After that the charge rises in steps. At 30 days or closer the venue often takes the whole expected sum.

The scale depends on the venue. In some places the last step comes at 30 days, in others at 7.

## A permitted shortfall

Many venues have one more line: a minimum guarantee. The group must pay for, say, 80 % of the booked places. If fewer come, you pay the difference anyway.

In hotel contracts the threshold is usually 70–90 % of the block. So 10–20 % of places can be "released" without a charge. Retreat centres more often fix a minimum number of guests a few weeks before arrival.

## How guests get their money back

For retreats sold directly to guests, the last refund date varies a lot. For some it is 14 days before the start. For others, especially with travel abroad, it is 90 days.

Common elements of guest terms:

- a small non-refundable first payment;
- refunds on a scale up to the last date;
- after that date, a transfer to another retreat within a year;
- the right to pass the place to another person.

## The rule of two calendars

**The guests' last refund date must come before the venue's point of no return.** Then, by the time the venue starts charging you, guests no longer have the right to take everything back.

Between the two dates you also need room to resell a place. A freed place can be sold to a new guest, and that reduces the losses on both sides.

This is not legal advice. The venue's dates come from your contract; check the guest terms against the law of your country.

<Phase type="practice">

1. Take your venue's contract or a teaching scale. Up to 120 days — no charge; 120–60 — half the first payment. Then 60–30 — 50 % of the contract; under 30 — 100 %.
2. Draw a line on paper from 180 days to zero and mark the venue's steps on it.
3. Below it, mark your guest terms: up to which day a full refund, up to which a partial one, when only a transfer.
4. Shade the days where a guest can still take their money but the venue is already charging you.
5. Move your dates until no shaded days remain. Write the new terms in one line.

</Phase>
