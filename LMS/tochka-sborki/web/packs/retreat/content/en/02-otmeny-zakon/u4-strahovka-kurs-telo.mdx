## Who insures what

As of 2026-10, the picture is this.

**The leader.** Usually two kinds: general liability and professional liability for mistakes in your work. Territory matters: some policies cover trips abroad only for a short period or accept claims only in the policy's country. Read the terms before the retreat, not after.

**The venue.** Insures its building and its own liability. Ask before the contract whether it expects insurance from you, and which.

**Event cancellation.** A separate policy. Weather and the leader not showing up are usually paid extensions, and pandemics are more often excluded.

**The guest.** Travel insurance. It is more often strongly recommended than required. If you require it, say so in the terms in advance.

## Waivers

Guests are often asked to sign a waiver before physical practices. Its force is limited. In the UK you cannot exclude liability for death or injury caused by negligence in a contract with a consumer. In the US the rules differ by state, and gross negligence can almost nowhere be waived. A waiver does not replace care. This is not legal advice: check the liability rules for your country.

## The exchange rate

If the guest price is in one currency and the venue charges in another, the exchange difference is your risk. Leaders tell how a move of a few cents ate thousands of dollars of profit.

Options:

- agree with the venue in the currency guests pay in;
- build a currency reserve into the price, sized to how much your currency pair moves;
- fix the rate in advance through a bank, if the sums are large.

In a package holiday the price can be raised for the exchange rate only under a clause in the contract. If the rise is above 8 %, the guest may withdraw.

## The body

Demanding practices — breathwork, intense movement, strong emotions — and practices with touch need two things. Contraindications are named in advance, on the practice card. Consent to touch is asked for explicitly, and saying no costs the guest nothing.

Whether a practice suits a guest is decided by the guest and their doctor. The leader does not diagnose.

</Phase>

<Phase type="practice">

1. Make three columns: "I insure", "the venue insures", "the guest insures".
2. Sort the risks of your retreat into them. For example: an injury in a practice, a weather cancellation, a guest's missed flight, the leader falling ill, damage to the building.
3. Find the risk that landed in no column. That is the first question for an insurer.
4. Check which currency you pay the venue in and which you charge guests in. If they differ, set a reserve in percent.
5. List the practices with load or touch and check that each has its contraindications and a way of consent named.

</Phase>
