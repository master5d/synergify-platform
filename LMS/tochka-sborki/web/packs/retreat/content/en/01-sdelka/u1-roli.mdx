<Phase type="activation">

Picture a wedding. The couple gathered the guests, and the banquet hall provided tables, a kitchen and an evening. The day before, three guests did not come. The tables are laid, and the cook cooked for everyone.

**Who pays for the empty places at the table?** Do not answer right away. Think about which of the two learned about the empty places first, and which learned last.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the empty table. The hall lost nothing if the couple bought every place in advance. The couple lost nothing if the hall charged for each guest who came.

**An empty place never disappears. It is simply written into someone's contract.** A retreat works the same way: the leader and the venue share one and the same risk — that fewer guests turn up.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## Three parties, one week

A retreat is a program, a place and a group over several days in a row. The leader brings the program. The venue provides the place. The leader gathers the group, and each guest pays for it.

The leader holds the meaning and the people: the offer, the price, sales, the program, contact with guests. The venue holds the walls and the daily life: rooms, kitchen, halls, cleaning, often transfers. Guests' money usually goes to the leader, and the leader pays the venue.

## Where the duties overlap

There is one overlap, and it is the main one: **the number of guests.** It drives the leader's revenue, the venue's occupancy, the kitchen's shopping and the cleaning shifts. If fewer guests come than planned, someone pays for empty beds.

Who exactly is decided by the contract model. That is the first thing to find out before signing.

## Four contract models

**Full campus.** The leader pays for every place on the campus at a rate per guest, even if fewer come. The venue gets guaranteed revenue. Empty places are the leader's cost.

**Per guest with a minimum.** The leader pays the rate for each guest who comes, but no less than an agreed minimum. The risk of a short group is shared: below the minimum the leader pays, above it they do not.

**Buyout.** The leader pays a fixed sum for the venue on the dates and usually an extra amount per guest — for meals, for example. The leader knows the main cost in advance.

**A free place for the leader.** The venue gives the leader free accommodation once the group reaches a threshold. This is how a venue invites leaders who already have their own audience.

## Why this comes before the price

The guest price, the cash plan and the cancellation terms all depend on the model. With a full campus, every guest short hits the leader's profit directly. With per-guest pricing, the same shortfall partly falls on the venue.

**The course does not recommend a "best" model.** It teaches you to see who carries the empty place in each model, and to count it in advance.

<Phase type="practice">

Take your own retreat or invent a teaching one: 7 nights, 16 places, 12 guests came.

1. Write the four contract models in a column.
2. For each, answer in one sentence: who pays for the four empty places?
3. Mark the model in which your side carries less risk.
4. Mark the model in which the other side carries less risk.
5. Think about which model you and the other side could agree on, and why.

You will need the answers in the next lesson: there the same deal is worked out in the calculator.

</Phase>
