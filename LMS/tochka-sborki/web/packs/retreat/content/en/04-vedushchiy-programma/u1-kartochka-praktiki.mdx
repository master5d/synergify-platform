<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the two labels. The first sells a miracle, the second states the contents.

**A practice description for the guest and the venue is a list of contents, not a promise.** From it a person decides whether the practice suits them, and the venue decides which hall and how much time are needed.

</Phase>

## How not to write it

Practice catalogues often contain promises: the practice "will heal", "opens abilities", "gives answers to any question", "clears all suppressed emotions". Such words cannot be checked, and they create an expectation the practice is not obliged to meet.

The second common problem is that something important is missing. It does not say the practice involves touch. It does not say a breathwork practice is a heavy load. The duration is given "per pair", although a group has many pairs.

## Fields of an honest card

- **Name** — what you call it.
- **Format** — group, pair or solo.
- **Duration for the group** — how many minutes the whole group takes, not one pair.
- **Load** — low, medium or high: breath, movement, emotions.
- **Contraindications** — when the practice is skipped or done lightly.
- **Touch** — yes or no; if yes, how consent is asked and how to decline.
- **What it needs** — a hall, mats, music.
- **What the practice does not promise** — one honest sentence.
- **Source** — tradition, author, where you trained.

Template: [practice card](/retreat-materials/practice-card.en.md).

## Example: an intense breathwork practice

Format — group. Duration for the group — 45 minutes. Load high: rapid breathing, dizziness possible. A guest discusses contraindications with their own doctor; the teaching card lists pregnancy, epilepsy and recent surgery as reasons to decline or do it lightly. No touch. Does not promise changes in health.

## Example: a pair practice with touch

Format — pair. Duration: 10 minutes for each role in a pair. With a group of 16 people, eight pairs work at once and swap roles — 20 minutes, about 25 with the explanation. Load low, emotions can be strong. Touch: a hand on the shoulder. Consent is asked before the practice, declining needs no explanation, and instead of pairing up one can observe.

Whether a practice suits a guest is decided by the guest and their doctor. The leader does not diagnose.

<Phase type="practice">

1. Take two practices from your program.
2. Download the [card template](/retreat-materials/practice-card.en.md) and fill it in for each.
3. Check the duration: is it for the whole group, not for one person or pair?
4. Find promises in your old descriptions and replace them with one honest sentence "what the practice does not promise".
5. For the practice with touch, write down exactly how you will ask for consent and what someone who declines does.

</Phase>
