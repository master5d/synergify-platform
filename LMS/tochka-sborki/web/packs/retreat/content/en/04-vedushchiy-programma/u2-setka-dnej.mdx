<Phase type="activation">

Picture an orchestra where every instrument was given a solo in the same minute. Violins, trumpets, drums — all at once and loud.

**Each plays beautifully, but it is impossible to listen.** Think about what a day looks like when every practice is the main one.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the orchestra. Music is made not by the number of notes, but by pauses and alternation.

**A retreat program is a score, not a list.** Its value is in how load and rest follow each other.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## More is not better

A leader's first program is almost always overloaded. You want to give guests everything you can do. As a result, by the third day people are tired, skip practices and have no time to live through what they came for.

## The rhythm of the day

The kitchen sets the anchors of the day: breakfast, lunch, dinner. Practices fit between them. A good day alternates:

- one high-load practice, more often in the morning;
- a calm practice after it;
- free time — at least a couple of hours;
- a quiet evening, without intense practices.

## Partial days

The arrival day and the departure day are not full. Guests arrive tired around lunch. On the last day there is breakfast, a short closing and the road. Putting the main practices on them means losing them.

## Load across the week

The week is also worth seeing as a whole. If every day is high-load, the group will be worn out by midweek. Better one or two peak days with a gentler day between them.

## The grid as a tool

The grid is easy to build in a table: rows are hours, columns are days. The template already has the meals and a "load of the day" row: [program grid](/retreat-materials/program-grid.en.csv). Each cell is filled by a practice card from the previous lesson.

<Phase type="practice">

1. Download the [grid template](/retreat-materials/program-grid.en.csv).
2. Lay out your practices across the days, using the group duration from the cards.
3. For each day, fill in the "load of the day" row and the "free time" row.
4. Check the arrival day and the departure day: are there no main practices on them?
5. Find a day with high load twice in a row and move one practice.
