<Phase type="activation">

Picture a menu table for the week. The chef carefully listed the dishes, prices and each day's total. Only one day has no total — someone forgot to extend the formula.

**Nobody noticed until it was time to pay.** Think about which other lines in a retreat's food get lost just as quietly.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the table with one total missing. The mistake is not in the cooking but in the accounting.

**Retreat food is three questions: who eats what, how much it costs and who counted it.** The answers are needed before arrival, not at the first dinner.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## The form comes before the menu

Diets and allergies are asked about in advance, in writing, from each guest. On site it is too late: the kitchen has bought the food, and a guest is allergic to nuts.

The form is worth asking about:

- how the guest eats: everything, no meat, vegan, gluten-free;
- allergies and how dangerous they are;
- arrival and departure flights;
- consent to touch in practices and to photos;
- an emergency contact and insurance.

Template: [guest form](/retreat-materials/guest-form.en.md). Answers about food go to the chef, flights go to the arrivals list. Health and the emergency contact go to you and to whoever is responsible for safety on site.

## The edges of the days

A week is counted in meals, not days. If arrival is at lunch and departure after breakfast, six nights mean six breakfasts, six lunches and six dinners. One meal too many or too few means money and hungry guests.

## A base and options

A common choice is a plant-based menu with meat as a paid option, one dish a day. That makes things simpler for the kitchen, and the guest decides what to pay for. Options and their price are written into the menu in advance.

## The cost per guest

The price is set per meal per person. Each day's total is the sum of its three meals, and the week's total is the sum of the days. Work them out with a formula, not by eye. Teaching example: breakfast 12, lunch 18, dinner 15 dollars. For a week that is 270 per guest, and 5,400 for a group of 20.

If meals are not included in the venue's rate, enter the cost per guest in the calculator in the "Cost per guest" field. Then honest profit takes it into account right away.

<Phase type="practice">

1. Download the [guest form](/retreat-materials/guest-form.en.md) and the [menu for a week](/retreat-materials/menu-week.en.csv).
2. In the menu, mark the meals by your dates: which meal starts arrival and which ends departure.
3. Enter the price of each meal and check the total per guest for the week.
4. Add a column for paid options, if there are any.
5. Enter the total per guest in the calculator's "Cost per guest" field and see how honest profit changed.
