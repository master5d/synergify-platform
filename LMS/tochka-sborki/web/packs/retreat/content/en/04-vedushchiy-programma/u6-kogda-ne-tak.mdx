<Phase type="activation">

Picture a plane before take-off. The crew goes through the same checklist yet again: what to do if an engine fails, if a passenger feels unwell.

**Why repeat what almost never happens?** Think about when the crew has no time to think.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the crew's checklist. It is written on the ground, on a calm day, because in the air there is no time to decide.

**The plan for a bad day on a retreat is written before arrival.** On site it is only opened.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## Who decides

First of all, agree who makes decisions on the retreat if something goes wrong. The leader, the co-leader, the venue's representative. One person decides, the others help. Write down their phone number and the number of whoever stands in for them.

## A guest who feels unwell

The guest form already knows about their health and a close contact. On site you also need: a first-aid kit, the address of the nearest hospital, the local emergency number. The leader does not treat or diagnose. They call for help, tell the close contact, and write down what happened and when.

## A difficult guest

Someone disrupts sessions, argues with the group, breaks the rules. First — a conversation in private, calmly and without witnesses. The support is the group rules the guest saw in the booklet. If the behaviour is a danger to others, the guest may leave early. The terms for that are written in advance in the contract and the cancellation terms.

## A booking or the journey falls through

The venue mixed up the dates, the transfer did not come, a flight was cancelled. Keep at hand the contract and the correspondence with the venue, a backup transport contact, the arrival list. To the guests — a short message: what happened, what we are doing, when we will write again.

## Weather and natural events

Heavy rain cancels a walk, heat cancels a practice in the sun. Keep an indoor backup for every day. If the whole retreat is cancelled, the contract decides: the venue's point of no return and the guests' cancellation terms from module 02. Guest insurance is their protection, and it is also from module 02.

## What to tell the group

Briefly, truthfully and at once: what happened, what is already being done, what changes in the programme. No details about someone else's health. Silence worries people more than bad news.

## Afterwards

Write down what happened and how it was solved — the same day. These notes are needed for the conversation with the venue and for the terms next time (unit u5).

<SelfCheck id="c6"/>

</Phase>

<Phase type="practice">

1. Write down who decides on your retreat if something goes wrong, and who stands in for them. Two phone numbers.
2. For your venue, find the nearest hospital, the emergency number and where the first-aid kit is kept.
3. For each day of the programme, write down an indoor backup.
4. Write three ready-made messages to the group: a guest feels unwell, a walk is cancelled, the transfer is late.
5. Check whether the booklet has group rules and an early-departure term. If not, add them.

</Phase>
