Picture a sign with a guest's name in an arrivals hall. The person holding the sign stands at exit number seven. The guest came out through exit number three.

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the two exits. Nobody made a mistake. There was just no single shared meeting point.

**The first hour of a retreat sets the tone for the whole week.** Arrival logistics is the first practice a guest goes through with you.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## One date — one place

The most common mistake is dates in different places. One date in the letter to the guest, another in the flights table, a third in the venue contract. The more copies, the more mismatches.

The rule is simple: the arrival and departure dates live in one place — the contract with the venue. Everything else refers to them.

## The arrivals list

For each guest:

- flight, date and time of arrival, airport;
- who meets them and exactly where — exit, desk, landmark;
- the guest's phone on the way;
- flight, date and time of departure;
- the return transfer.

Template: [arrivals list](/retreat-materials/arrival-list.en.csv).

## A reserve for mistakes

Someone will mix up the time, someone will miss a connection. Leaders budget a reserve for a separate transfer — one or two per group. That is cheaper than a spoiled first day.

## A week before arrival

The arrivals list is built from the form and checked personally: a short message to each guest with their time and meeting point. It is also the last point of contact before arrival.

<Phase type="practice">

1. Download the [arrivals list](/retreat-materials/arrival-list.en.csv).
2. Enter the arrival and departure dates from the venue contract — only from there.
3. Fill in the guests' flights from the form.
4. For each guest, name the exact meeting point: exit, desk, landmark.
5. Add a "transfer reserve" line to the budget and enter it in the calculator as a fixed cost.

</Phase>
