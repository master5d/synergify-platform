<Phase type="activation">

Picture a menu where one dish has "plus 50" written in the corner. The chef knows it is meat at an extra charge. The guest sees a mysterious number.

**One line, clear to one and unclear to the other.** Think about which of them will come to you with a question at dinner.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the "plus 50" note. It is not a mistake; it was simply never agreed out loud.

**Meals are a part of the package that the venue and the leader must describe the same way.** Otherwise the argument starts at the serving table.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## Price per meal

Meals are easiest to count per guest per meal: breakfast, lunch, dinner. Then any week can be built from the bricks, and changes in the group show up in money right away.

## The edges of the week

How many meals are in the package depends on arrival and departure. With arrival at lunch and departure after breakfast, six nights mean six breakfasts, six lunches and six dinners. This is written into the contract explicitly.

## A base and options

A common choice for venues is a plant-based menu with meat as a paid option, one dish a day. The option's price is on the menu, and the guest decides in advance, through the form.

## The menu is a document

A menu for the chef is a table: day, meal, dishes, a vegan and gluten-free option, allergens, options, price. Each day's total is calculated automatically, the week's total is the sum of the days. A missing total for one day is a classic quiet mistake in such tables.

Template: [menu for a week](/retreat-materials/menu-week.en.csv).

## When to agree it

The venue gets the final menu and the number of guests with diets from the leader before the shopping. That is usually a couple of weeks before arrival. That deadline is worth writing into the contract, next to the final guest count.
