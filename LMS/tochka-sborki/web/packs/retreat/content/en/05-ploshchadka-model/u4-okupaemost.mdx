<Phase type="activation">

Picture a spreadsheet where the investment almost pays back in the very first year and returns eight times over seven years. The formulas are filled down, the cells are green, the charts go up.

**The spreadsheet looks convincing precisely because it was calculated.** Think about what could be wrong with it if the formulas make no mistakes.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Go back to the green cells. A formula honestly multiplies what it is given. The mistake lives not in the arithmetic but in what exactly is being multiplied.

**A payback spreadsheet deceives its own author most quietly of all.** The author entered the assumptions and then believed the result.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## A teaching model

Download the [glamping payback model](/retreat-materials/payback-model-8-errors.en.csv). It has 6 units, a night price, occupancy, meals, yoga packages, expenses and "payback". The first year shows a 76 % return, the third 240 %.

**It contains eight mistakes.** Try to find them yourself before reading on.

## Eight mistakes

1. **The wrong investment.** The return is divided by 400,000, while the estimate on the next sheet gives 630,000.
2. **Revenue booked as profit.** Yoga package revenue goes into "ROI" in full, with no teacher's pay, hall or marketing.
3. **Occupancy counted twice.** The number of meal guests is already multiplied by occupancy, and meal revenue is multiplied by it again.
4. **The wrong label.** The "vacancy rate" line means the share left empty, but the model has occupancy under it.
5. **Profit halved without explanation.** It looks like a partner's share, but it is never named.
6. **Words used in the wrong sense.** "ROI" here is a cumulative sum of money, and "cap rate" is that cumulative sum divided by the investment. A real cap rate is annual net profit over the value of the property.
7. **An arbitrary factor.** All expenses are multiplied by 0.8, including insurance and permits.
8. **Missing lines without which payback is not calculated.** Taxes, depreciation and equipment replacement, debt service, seasonality.

## Why this matters before investing

Each mistake on its own looks small. Together they turn a project returning a few percent into "it almost pays back in a year". Investment decisions are made from the spreadsheet, so the spreadsheet is checked before the decision.

<Phase type="practice">

1. Download the [teaching model](/retreat-materials/payback-model-8-errors.en.csv) and find the eight mistakes using the "How it is calculated" column.
2. Correct the investment to 630,000 and recalculate the first-year return.
3. Take yoga package revenue out of profit, or subtract the teacher's costs from it.
4. Remove the double occupancy from meals.
5. Write down what first-year return is left after these three corrections. It should come out at about 7 % instead of 76 %, and that is still without taxes, depreciation and honest expenses.

</Phase>
