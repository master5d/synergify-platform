## What occupancy is

Occupancy is the share of occupied nights: occupied nights divided by units × days in the month. It gets confused with the share of empty nights. In English spreadsheets you will meet the label "vacancy rate" with occupancy actually sitting under it. Check what exactly is multiplied into revenue.

## Two mixes for one year

A venue has two ways to fill its nights. Nightly letting — a guest for one to three nights, at the night price. A retreat week — a group for a week, at a rate per guest.

Teaching example: 6 units, a night at 140 dollars, one week.

- **Nightly at 60 % occupancy:** 6 × 7 × 140 × 0.6 = 3,528 dollars for the week.
- **A retreat for 12 guests at 900 per guest per week:** 10,800 dollars of revenue. Meals and kitchen work come out of it, which nightly letting does not have.

## The hidden cost of a group

Retreat weeks have their own lines:

- empty days between groups for cleaning and preparation;
- a kitchen and staff for the whole week;
- halls and equipment;
- a group cancelled a month before arrival — a week that can no longer be sold night by night.

So you compare not revenue but the month's result after costs.

## Seasonality

Retreat weeks usually take the high and middle season, and nightly letting fills the gaps. An honest year model shows occupancy by month, not one number for the whole year. And occupancy does not grow by itself year after year: each year is worked out from last year's real sales.

<Phase type="practice">

1. Download the [venue year template](/retreat-materials/venue-year.en.csv).
2. For each month, enter real or cautious occupancy, not one number for the whole year.
3. Mark the months where you can realistically sell retreat weeks, and how many.
4. For those months, work out the result of two options: nightly letting only, and retreats plus nightly letting in the gaps.
5. Account for empty days between groups and kitchen costs. Write down which mix gives the best result for the year.
