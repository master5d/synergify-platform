<Phase type="activation">

Recall how the word "mindfulness" sounds in ordinary speech around you. Not in this course — in posts, in app advertising, in conversation over a table.

Try to reconstruct what stands behind the word in those uses. Most often something smooth, pleasant and private: a candle, a mat, a quiet morning, "I don't react to other people's emotions".

**Notice what is missing from that picture.** Not one other person. Not one piece of news. Not one circumstance you cannot influence.

Don't draw conclusions yet. Just hold that strangely empty room in mind.

</Phase>

<Phase type="reflection">

Change the lens. Not a room, but **noise-cancelling headphones.**

A useful object. On a plane, indispensable. But it has a property visible only from outside: the person wearing them doesn't become calmer, they become **less informed**. Outside there is exactly the same roar; the only thing that changed is whether it reaches them.

**Notice the difference between "it got quieter" and "I stopped hearing".** From inside they feel identical. They can be told apart by one sign only: what happens when the headphones come off.

This step is about a practice that quietly turns into headphones.

</Phase>

<Phase type="concept">

## Two different things called by one word

The first: noticing what is there — including the unpleasant, including your own place in what's happening and your own part in it.

The second: **achieving a smooth state in which the unpleasant doesn't reach you.**

Both are called "mindfulness". The second is not a caricature and not a rare case; it is the standard mass usage of the word. And it describes not a practice of attention but a psychological defence: avoidance, formatted as spiritual work.

A defence, like any defence, has its moments of fitness. But it has a price, and the price is worth knowing in advance.

## What exactly gets pushed out

Three things, and all three regularly:

- **The social.** What is happening to the people around you and to the country you live in. It is the first thing to leave the picture of "a mindful life".
- **Your own part.** Not "what was done to me" but "what I take part in". A practice that works only with sensations never raises this.
- **Response as such.** Anxiety, fear, rage and a sense of isolation in reaction to real events are **a healthy response to what is happening, not a fault to be meditated away.** A complete absence of response in hard circumstances is sometimes fitting and adequate, but that too is a defence, not an achievement.

## A sense of one's own importance, included

Sealing over often comes with a curious by-product: a feeling of being advanced. Not "I'm training attention" but "I see more clearly than others, because I work on myself".

The best-known analysis of this mechanism is Ken Wilber's **"Boomeritis Buddhism"**: how contemporary New Age spirituality fuses with narcissism and egocentrism, and how the language of "working on yourself" becomes a justification for never stepping outside yourself. An honest caveat: **this is philosophical polemic, not research.** It is named here as the source of a frame and as an optic, not as evidence.

A practical sign visible without any theory: if after practice it has become easier to judge the people around you — "they're not mindful", "he's being reactive", "she should breathe" — then what's being trained isn't attention.

## How this looks in courses

There is a recognisable construction. A format with no conversation between people; where a substantive question disappears into a feedback form; where everything difficult a person brings comes back to them as advice to "seek professional help if you can't cope". Formally impeccable — and arranged so that the difficult never becomes shared.

One item from the safety literature lands here exactly: if resistance and refusal are read as the student's problem, that is the very interpretive frame which, in *Transcultural Psychiatry* (2022), lengthens difficulties. Step five was about this at the level of a single session; here it is the same thing at the level of how a course is built.

## What practice without sealing over does

It doesn't remove the response and doesn't promise comfort. It adds to the response a gap one breath wide — the same gap as in step four. Inside that gap you can **choose** a reply instead of emitting one. The difference between "I feel no anxiety" and "I feel anxiety and can do something with it" is the whole substance of this step.

And a consequence that leads straight into step eight: **if practice raises what concerns other people, then it gets worked through with people.** Nobody exits sealing over alone — because from inside it looks like peace.

</Phase>

<Phase type="practice">

**Session — 15 minutes.** Today the practice is deliberately not private: the second half includes what is going on around you.

1. Sit. Eyes open, soft gaze. Timer — 15 minutes.
2. The first five minutes: breath, as in step two. No additions.
3. The next five minutes: **the sounds of the room and the street.** Don't select the pleasant ones, don't wall off the unpleasant ones. Just let them reach you. If a wish "not to hear" appears, label it with one word (step four) and stay.
4. The next three minutes: **one person.** Take a specific living person you know and hold them in the field of attention. Don't solve their problems and don't work out what to advise them. Just let them be there.
5. If heaviness rises during those three minutes, the rules of step five apply: label, breathe, and at any stop signal end the session.
6. The last two minutes: breath only again.
7. At the chime, stand and look out of the window.

After the session, record in the journal: which sounds you wanted to shut out, what happened in the body when a living person entered the field, and whether there is a subject in your life right now that practice helps you not to notice.

**Talking about the session.** Press "Learn with AI" and hand it the last of those three answers — it's the most uncomfortable one. A good question for the companion: "what objections to this come to me first?" Objections are worth more than agreement here. And keep in mind a limit we'll examine in the next step: an interlocutor trained to agree is a poor partner in exactly this conversation, which is why it will really happen in a circle, in step eight.

</Phase>
