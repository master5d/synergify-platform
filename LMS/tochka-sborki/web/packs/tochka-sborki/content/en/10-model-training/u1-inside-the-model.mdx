## Tokens: how the model sees text

The model doesn't read letters or words. Text is cut into **tokens** — pieces of words, sometimes whole words, sometimes single characters. Each token maps to a number in the model's vocabulary.

Hence the everyday oddities: the model is bad at counting letters in a word and gets confused by long numbers. It sees chunks, not letters.

## Embeddings: meaning as coordinates

Each token number turns into an **embedding** — a long vector of numbers. Pieces with similar meaning land close together in this space. The same idea powers semantic search in your AI notebook (module 09) and the second brain (module 11).

## The transformer and attention

The core mechanism of modern models is described in the paper ["Attention Is All You Need"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) (Vaswani et al., 2017). The architecture is called the **transformer**.

The gist of **attention**: for each token, the model decides which other tokens in the text to look at, and how strongly. In "the cat didn't fit in the box because it was too small," the word "it" should look at "box."

There are many attention layers, and each refines the picture. Nothing else is inside: no fact database, no truth check.

## Pretraining: predict the next token

The model learns one task: **predict the next token** from the previous ones. It is shown a huge text corpus and guesses the continuation billions of times. Each mistake nudges its numbers slightly — the **weights**, or **parameters**.

This is **pretraining**. It produces a base model — an excellent text continuer, but not yet an assistant. **Post-training** makes it one: further training on "question → good answer" examples and tuning to human preferences.

## Why the model knows — and why it lies

To continue text well, it pays for the model to capture the regularities of the world: grammar, facts, styles of reasoning. That's why it "knows."

But the training goal is a **plausible** continuation, not a **true** one. Where data is scarce or contradictory, plausible and true diverge. The model continues smoothly — and is confidently wrong.

That's why the trick from modules 09 and 11 works. When a source sits in the context, the most plausible continuation is the one that leans on the source.
